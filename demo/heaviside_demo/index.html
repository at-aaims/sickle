Heaviside Reconstruction Lab

A small U-Net rebuilds a 1024-point field from a handful of samples. It was trained on randomized fields, so you can change the wavenumber, amplitude and sample count and watch it reconstruct, running live in your browser. Blue bands on the sliders show the training range. Past them, the model is extrapolating.

Waves travel at sickle's speeds while playing: the background at 0.2 and the burst at 0.5 domain lengths per unit time. The U-Net (archs/unet1d.py, channels 16/32/64/128, 711k parameters) takes the linear interpolation, the sample mask and the sample values, and predicts a correction to the interpolation. It was trained with every field drawn at random: k from 2 to 32, A from 0 to 1.5, any r₀, k₀ from 0.5 to 3, A₀ from 0 to 1.5, M from 8 to 256, and noise up to σ = 0.05 on half the fields.

Source: demo/heaviside_demo/ on the heaviside-demo branch of sickle.
